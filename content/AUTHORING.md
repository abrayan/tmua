# Preparing a guided TMUA paper

Treat this document as the standing authoring and review prompt. Complete the mathematical review before publishing. The aim is to develop the student's ability to choose and carry out a method independently, including on a new question.

## Sources and scope

Use the current official TMUA specification as the content boundary. The 2026 specification is linked in `content/syllabus-coverage.json`; Section 1 mathematics applies to both papers, and Section 2 adds reasoning. Keep solutions within that specification, using elementary algebra, graphs, geometry and the permitted calculus. Do not require Further Mathematics, induction, formal truth tables or an unnecessarily advanced method.

Read the actual question, all options, diagrams, domain and endpoint conventions. Obtain its official answer key and worked answer when available. Work through the argument yourself as well: official worked answers can contain slips or omitted boundary cases. For a third-party or newly authored question, independently derive the answer and check it with a second method, exact algebra, exhaustive finite enumeration or boundary checks as appropriate. Preserve attribution and publication permissions.

## Freely published JZ Mock originals

Public Mock A–E originals use `provider: "jzmaths-mock"` and `sourceId` such as `JZ-MOCK-A-P1-Q01`. A complete paper uses `id: "jz-mock-a-p1"`, `pairId: "jz-a"`, `paper: 1`, `visibility: "public"`, twenty originals in Q01–Q20 order and the usual adaptive practice policy. Both metadata and each original retain JZ Mock attribution and the exact source URL `https://jzmaths.com/papers/jz_mocks/jz_mock_a_p1_question.pdf`, with only the published mock letter and paper number varied. Keep the corresponding `jz-mock-a-p1-plan.json` under `content/`; public plan discovery audits it with the official plans. These are third-party originals, never relabelled official TMUA. The public Mock allowance does not admit purchased Exam Sets, simulator URLs or JZ questions as follow-up candidates. Follow-ups still use reviewed official TMUA sources first, with the existing explicitly reviewed fallback policy.

## Purchased sources and private delivery

Purchased content must remain outside this public repository: questions, diagrams, authored solutions, private audit records and compiled HTML all stay in a private authoring directory. Preserve the third-party provider and exact question identity; never present it as an official TMUA question. Use [the private pair compiler](../tools/PRIVATE-COMPILATION.md) and its full review gates, then [the private release procedure](../supabase/PRIVATE-GUIDED.md). A release still contains both papers and requires separate QA. Private release metadata and versioned mappings are append-only in the private database; do not add paid payloads to the public edition manifests. Ordinary content preparation cannot change access rules or schema. The separately authorized private-delivery migration must be reviewed before deployment.

## Review every exercise, including follow-ups

1. Record the exact source ID and source page. Check that the displayed question crop includes every condition and all answer choices, with readable diagrams. Use the established crop wrapper `<img class="source-question" tabindex="0" role="button" aria-label="Enlarge question" src="…" alt="Descriptive question and options text">` for every source image, including follow-ups; it provides responsive sizing and keyboard enlargement. Preserve the complete image. In both interfaces on mobile, verify that enlargement shows readable intrinsic source dimensions, that keyboard and touch scrolling can reach the right edge and final option, and that Back to practice stays reachable and restores focus to the source image.
2. Derive the answer. Check domains, zero denominators, sign changes, equality cases, degeneracy, extraneous roots, repeats and quantifier scope where relevant. Record a question-specific verification, not just “checked”. Resolve any disagreement between your derivation, the key and the official explanation explicitly. If the source wording admits different answers, do not silently use the publisher’s intended reading. Preserve the source and add a short clarification before the student answers only when the intended mathematical task is established by the worked explanation. Record the exact ambiguity, clarification and source evidence, and have a separate reviewer approve the clarified task. If the intended task or answer remains unresolved, hold the release. When a question or its options continue on another page, include the continuation in the same exercise and verify its final option in the enlarged reader.
3. Check every line of the full solution. Keep it complete, concise and accessible. The explanation must identify why the chosen method applies, not merely give the selected letter. Give the knowledge recap even after a correct answer.
4. Write a progressive hint ladder. Each hint supplies one useful fact, representation or next step and ends with a specific task the student can do on paper. The first hint helps the student recognise the structure; later hints add only what is needed if still stuck. Use the booklet lesson reminder only when it genuinely teaches that step.
5. Read the hints cumulatively as a student. Audit the title, body, recap, pitfall, reminder and pause text together: any of these can leak the answer. Never give the answer letter, requested final number, complete solution set, truth classification or an equivalent expression that merely needs matching to an option. For a counting question, do not state the count. For a necessity/sufficiency question, do not announce which directions hold. The last hint must leave a meaningful mathematical task, not just selecting the already-revealed result. Put completed calculations and conclusions in the full solution. Treat a fully enumerated recurrence cycle, solved parameter bounds, a named law when that name is requested, or a substituted numerical formula needing only routine arithmetic as an answer leak too. Remove enough supplied intermediate work that the student still has to choose or execute the decisive mathematical step. Short questions may need only one or two hints; do not add a final hint simply to fill three slots.
6. Make pitfalls specific to a likely mistake in this question. A warning must not quietly solve the question. Distinguish a valid alternative method from an error.
7. Link every genuinely tested concept, using stable IDs from `assets/studied-concepts.json`. Do not tag routine incidental arithmetic as assessed knowledge. Distinguish the teaching reminder from the concepts actually assessed. Use new syllabus-backed concepts when necessary, without inventing booklet references. Record a reason for each question's links.
8. Independently solve and review every similar question too. Prefer a genuinely comparable question from another official TMUA paper. It must exercise the intended knowledge and have compatible prerequisites; sharing a broad topic is insufficient. Do not use an exact reprint or a question from the current assessment. Choose up to three distinct follow-ups. Distinguish direct matches from useful prerequisite transfers and record that relationship precisely. If a third defensible match cannot be found, offer fewer and document the gap in the review rather than using filler or promising three. Set the reviewed plan's authoring count policy accordingly; preserve frozen earlier editions.

Read the actual knowledge statements behind every proposed concept ID and lesson reminder. Match the operation the student performs, not a broadly related title. Counting graph intersections does not automatically assess derivative-based turning-height analysis; expanding powers does not automatically assess factor cancellation; an integer minimum is not necessarily optimisation over a substituted continuous interval. A reminder must point to a lesson that teaches that specific fact. In a follow-up rationale, distinguish work both questions require from extra work required only by the follow-up, and distinguish a sum used inside one sequence term from the sequence being summed. If checking a reused question reveals an incorrect mapping or a hint that identifies the requested first error, repair it through a reviewed teaching edition and renew every affected relationship instead of carrying the defect into a new paper.

## Evidence and acceptance checks

### Plain-language hints from worked explanations

For JZ papers, begin with JZ's own current worked solution. Review a linked teacher's video explanation as well: read the explanation and inspect the on-screen working wherever diagrams, notation or a transcription error could change the method. Record the video URL and the relevant timestamps. Confirm that the video solves the same version of the question; a matching question number is not enough. Use the current publisher solution when a video predates a correction. Do not describe a transcript-only review as watching the complete video, and do not copy a tutor's mathematical slip or an unsupported shortcut.

Choose one clear, syllabus-level method and use it consistently in the hints and the full solution. Name the quantity or expression the student should work on. Prefer “Write the first three terms of each sequence” to “Identify the sequence structure”, or “Find the gradient between the two centres” to “Exploit the geometric relationship”. Explain why a step helps in a short sentence. Introduce any new symbol before using it. Avoid compressed labels such as “admissible branch”, “boundary criterion”, “truth signature” or “coefficient selection” when a simple instruction will do.

Each reveal should contain one small step. Read its title, body, reminder, recap, warning and return-to-question instruction together; remove repeated explanations and avoid making the student read a paragraph to find the action. The last line should ask for a concrete piece of work on paper. Leave the decisive calculation or reasoning to the student. Do not replace an unclear hint with an entire worked answer. Keep the same language when the same mathematical move appears in a later question, while naming that question's actual quantities.

The independent reviewer must answer two additional questions for every revised ladder: “Would the student know what to write next?” and “What mathematics must the student still do?” A correct but cryptic ladder fails the review. Record source evidence, any disagreement between the video and publisher, the chosen method and the clarity verdict with the question-specific audit. Do not publish full third-party transcripts or private student progress as review evidence.

Test clarity against the actual screens. Before answering, the student sees the title, body, lesson reminder, warning and return instruction. After answering, they see the recap, reminder and warning. Give these fields distinct purposes; repeating the same explanation in several boxes makes a short hint feel long. The body should lead with the next thing to write or draw. Do not hide three tasks in a sentence joined by “then”, or end with “justify” when the student has not been given a usable way to check the claim. For a graphical equation, “Sketch the two graphs on the same axes. Each intersection is a solution” can be a better starting point than an algebraic transformation. Name the actual functions when the question supplies them. Use a later hint only when it adds a useful next move.

Record the verification, verified answer, hint verdict, specific work left after the final hint, concept IDs, mapping reason, source references and issues in `content/question-audits.json`. Record a fingerprint of the reviewed mathematical/teaching fields. The audit validator must reject missing, duplicate, unknown, incomplete or stale records. Update a record only after reviewing the changed material, never merely to make a check pass. The concept audit and syllabus crosswalk must identify the actual knowledge supporting each numbered clause; catalogue coverage does not mean the student has mastered it.

Record each final follow-up relationship in `content/followup-audits.json`, including its reason, direct/transfer verdict, genuinely shared concept IDs and fingerprint of both questions plus the authored match. Run the follow-up audit gate so changes to either question or the matching rationale trigger a new review.

Review rendered maths and diagrams on desktop and mobile. Test first answers, hint use before answering, incremental hint disclosure, checking an answer only after selecting an option, full recap, returning to the question, similar exercises and finishing the paper. Check that no solution is visible on the question screen and that opening a recap after a correct answer does not reduce its score.

Preserve each account's original answers, all numbered attempts, manually recorded results and scores. Existing published files and teaching editions remain immutable. Publish teaching corrections as a new reviewed edition with safe routing: new attempts use the latest edition, and a saved attempt stays with the edition it began with. Never silently resume an old attempt using changed answers, hint counts or follow-up order. Do not change scoring rules or cloud permissions during a content update.

Publish new full papers only in complete Paper 1 / Paper 2 pairs. Run content/audit, player, account-isolation, browser and build checks. Verify the deployment after pushing. Report exactly which papers/exercises were reviewed, which changes were made and any unresolved mathematical or source limitation. Do not claim that a catalogue, a guided score or one paper guarantees an examination score or readiness.

## Independent review before every push

Hold the release for a QA reviewer who did not write the changes. Give that reviewer the exact sources and current staged files, not just the author's summaries. The reviewer must independently solve every changed original and follow-up, read every complete hint ladder, check the assessed concept links against the actual work, and assess every affected follow-up relationship. A broad topic in common does not make two questions variations of the same task: mark a narrower skill transfer honestly or reject the match.

Record question-specific findings and the exact reviewed fingerprints. Fix findings, then have the reviewer read the corrected material again. Renew the dependent relationship reviews when teaching or matching changes. Review all rendered teaching fields for missing inequalities, malformed maths and clipped diagrams. A passed fingerprint gate proves review freshness, not mathematical or pedagogical correctness.

Use local browser tests for normal and Pearson views, mobile layouts, answer selection, hints, recap, redo, adaptive follow-ups, finishing, reload/resume, numbered attempts, account changes, manager read-only viewing, and the complete archive/remove/restore lifecycle. Check that hiding or restoring a pair never deletes its questions, answers, attempts or scores. Exercise upload validation and recovery where supported. Do not submit test answers to the live student's account.

Keep an explicit coverage matrix: passed, failed, not implemented, or not verified. Do not describe an unimplemented upload worker, absent PDF-management action or mocked service test as a working production feature. Any unresolved defect affecting the release blocks publication. Preserve the original assessment records and edition-specific teaching when correcting content or concept mappings; never assign new knowledge credit to an old answer without question-level evidence.

### Freeze reviewed sources with each published edition

The authoring question bank may advance without changing a paper the student has already started or completed. A release can update one complete pair while shared official questions stay unchanged in other published papers. Their current concept mappings continue to match their latest published HTML; do not copy a revised bank mapping into an unrelated paper without publishing and reviewing a new edition of that paper.

After preparing the selected editions and their mapping version, run `npm run editions:freeze-sources`. It appends `content/edition-source-snapshots.json` and content-addressed records under `content/edition-sources/`. Each source snapshot binds the exact original lock, used questions, full hint ladders, solutions, question/concept audits, lesson catalogue and follow-up plan/reviews to its compiled edition. Question records are shared by hash to avoid duplicating image data. Never modify a previous snapshot, binding or question record. The first migration may bootstrap an existing latest edition only from the committed source baseline and only when recompilation reproduces its exact bytes; it is not a new content review.

Run `npm run audit:editions` before publishing. New snapshots must match current reviewed inputs; existing snapshots must reproduce their own immutable edition and match the previous committed registry. The gate also checks every prior original, edition file and manifest record. A changed compiler must still reproduce frozen sources or receive an explicit, separately reviewed migration; do not reset snapshot hashes to pass. Production validation reads its own Git HEAD. Isolated QA may supply an explicit trusted baseline reader and must report that scope. Existing authored-source and follow-up gates still review the entire current bank; immutable snapshot validation is an additional release requirement.

### Preserve concept evidence when correcting mappings

Question-to-concept corrections belong to a new reviewed teaching edition. Keep every existing entry in `assessmentMappings.versions` and `assessmentMappings.editionBindings` immutable. The initial `published-dbb92eb` version preserves the exact previously published mapping; do not reconstruct it from corrected teaching.

After independently reviewing the question mappings, update only the current `papers` mappings for papers receiving a new teaching edition in this release, append those reviewed teaching editions, and run `node tools/freeze-concept-mappings.mjs NEW_UNIQUE_VERSION_ID`. This freezes the corrected map and binds only newly appended editions. Run `npm run audit:mappings` along with the content and follow-up gates. Never repoint an existing edition binding or refresh a frozen mapping hash. Commit the new `content/concept-mapping-locks.json` entries with the release.

Historical snapshots resolve their own saved `teachingEdition` (or `original` for old snapshots without a pin). Repeated exposure is tracked across editions and exact reprints, so a new mapping never earns fresh independent credit for a repeated question. An unknown answered edition must show that concept progress is unavailable instead of guessing; saved answers and raw paper scores remain unchanged. Keep the same numerical evidence weights. Verify student and manager views, reloads, new attempts, old attempts and account changes with the provenance regressions before publishing.

### Recall for knowledge beyond the booklets

Use a reviewed `additionalConcepts` ID for newly discovered syllabus knowledge. Resolve its title and paper from the catalogue and label the reminder **New learning**; do not add a booklet, lesson number or page that does not exist. Use properly typeset maths in both progressive reminders and the full correct-answer recap. The reviewed-edition compiler may upgrade the recognised recall renderer in a new edition only; every original and previous edition remains frozen. Run the additional-recall browser and compiler checks whenever changing this rendering path.


When a hint applies a definition supplied by the question or explains a step for which the catalogue has no honest lesson reference, it may omit the reminder. Keep the mathematical instruction in the hint itself. The question audit must contain one `recallOmissions` entry for each such hint: its one-based `hintNumber`, a specific `reason`, `status: "pass"`, separate `author` and `reviewer`, and the exact question `contentHash`. An empty reminder without that independent review fails the content gate. Never substitute an unrelated lesson to satisfy a field. This does not excuse missing assessed concepts or missing syllabus teaching; add reviewed additional concepts when the question directly assesses new syllabus knowledge.

## Draw first when the picture supplies the method

Audit every original and follow-up for a useful representation before writing its hint ladder. Decide from the actual task, options and diagram, not from graph-related keywords. Record whether a sketch is essential, helpful or unnecessary, what the student should draw or add, and how it helps answer the question.

When the route depends on seeing a graph or a geometric relationship, the first useful hint must name a concrete action: sketch the two functions on the same axes, mark the roots and decide whether the graph crosses or touches, draw the boundary lines, shade the required region, add a radius or perpendicular, or draw a probability tree. If the question already supplies the picture, ask the student to annotate or use it rather than copy it without purpose. Do not start with abstract algebraic language when the picture makes the method easier to see. Do not force a drawing onto a question with a clearer short algebraic solution.

Keep hints short and sharp: normally one instruction in one or two short sentences. Replace an unclear hint instead of adding a paragraph to it. Do not repeat the same action in the body, recap and return-to-question line. Give the student space to carry out the step before offering another.

Keep the learning sequence recognisable across questions: **draw or mark → read the mathematical relationship → calculate or justify → check the result**. For f(x)=g(x), say that each intersection gives a solution; for inequalities, say what above/below means; for an area, identify the boundaries before integrating. Give only the next needed piece in each hint. Where a rough sketch cannot establish an exact count, boundary or maximum, retain the elementary algebra/sign/monotonicity argument that makes the conclusion reliable. A drawing is a route into the proof, not a substitute for it.

Use the exact relevant studied-booklet lesson reminder so a familiar skill is reused. State new knowledge plainly and label it New learning when no studied lesson contains it. A reminder is not automatically an assessed-concept link: assess only knowledge actually needed in the question. In the full solution and correct-answer recap, state briefly what the diagram helped reveal so the student can recognise that move next time.

Independent QA must inspect the entire ladder cumulatively and ask: **Does the student know what to draw, why it helps, and what to do next?** Do not reveal the decisive arrangement, intersection count, requested coordinates or shaded answer through a hint, title, pitfall or reminder. Leave that work to the student. Review questions already attempted separately from unstarted ones; preserve every saved attempt and its teaching edition.
